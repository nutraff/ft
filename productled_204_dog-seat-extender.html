My Dog Finally Had Room to Lie Down on Long Drives

Real solutions for real pet problems · FurTrouble

2024 11 15

My dog rides in the back seat, and on every turn she slides. On highway ramps she slides hard, ending up with her back legs in the footwell and her front half on the seat, looking betrayed. I'd reach back and haul her up, she'd settle, the next turn would come, and we'd do it again for four hours. Long drives were exhausting for both of us, and I'm pretty sure she dreaded the car.

The root problem is the gap. The space between the front seats and the back seat is open, and a dog standing or lying across the back has nothing to brace against when the car moves. A harness keeps her from flying forward in a stop, but it doesn't stop the sideways slide, and it doesn't give her a flat surface to actually lie down on.

Why This Is a Safety Issue, Not Just Comfort

A dog that's sliding around is a distraction. Every time I reached back to reposition her, my eyes left the road. And a dog wedged in a footwell during a sudden stop is at real risk of injury, even with a harness, because the harness is designed for forward deceleration, not for being thrown sideways into a gap. I kept telling myself I'd deal with it later. Then she got older and stiffer, and getting herself back onto the seat became a struggle that made me nervous every time.

What I Tried First

First I put a folded blanket in the footwell to raise the floor. It helped for about ten minutes before she kicked it aside and the gap was back. Then a dog hammock that hung between the front and back headrests. It created a flat surface but it also blocked her view, made her restless, and was a pain to install and remove every time. It also didn't solve the sliding, it just gave her a softer thing to slide on.

I tried a booster seat, which is designed for small dogs and was absurd for a 60-pound dog. Then I considered a crate in the back, but my car's cargo area is too small and she gets anxious in enclosed spaces. I needed something that filled the gap without enclosing her.

What Actually Worked

A seat extender is a platform that spans the gap between the front and back seats, creating one continuous flat surface. The version I use has a hard panel that rests on the front seat backs and the rear seat, with a soft cover on top. It installs in about thirty seconds and comes out just as fast. She now has a full flat bed across the back of the car and can lie down, shift, and brace herself without falling into anything.

The first drive with it was a two-hour highway trip. She lay down within five minutes and slept most of the way. I didn't reach back once. On curves she shifts slightly but stays on the surface. I added a non-slip seat cover over the whole thing for traction and to protect the upholstery, and now road trips are actually pleasant. She even gets excited when I open the car door, which she never did before.
